Case studies

Each case study covers the problem, my role, the methods I chose and why, and what changed because of the research.

Diagnosing Drop-Off in a Credit Card Feature

Nearly 70% of users who entered a credit card feature left before the final page. Research traced it to the wrong cards being displayed with no good way to fix them. Engineering fixed the data problem, drop-off improved, and card editing was added.

Identifying Significant Roadblocks to New Product Adoption

Prior to a product launch, research showed that a new product would not give users more value than they already receive elsewhere. Product and development work was cancelled, likely saving months of time and effort.

→

Finding Friction in a Long-Requested Feature

Users long asked to import their insurance policy, and a prototype delivered it. After launch, forgotten logins and 1-5+ minute waits caused abandonment. Stakeholders observed the delays and worked to fix them.

→

Iterating on an Overlooked Personalization Touchpoint

Users ignored a loan-offer personalization carousel. Four research methods in two weeks narrowed many designs to one that was twice as effective in an A/B test. 78,000+ users have since completed it.

How I work

Methodologies

Every project starts with one question to stakeholders: what are you trying to learn? The answer decides the method. Open any method to see what it is and when I choose it.

I also use AI to organize evidence, surface patterns, and make past research easier to find. I treat its output as a first pass and check it against the source evidence before it becomes a finding.

Test the experience

Does this design work for users?

Moderated usability testing

Live sessions where users think aloud as they use a prototype or product, with follow-up questions on what I observe.

I reach for it when we need to understand why users struggle, not just where.

Unmoderated testing

Users complete tasks on their own time while their screens and voices are recorded.

I reach for it when the questions are focused and we need answers within days.

Competitive usability testing

Running usability sessions on a competitor's product to learn what works and what frustrates users.

I reach for it when we're entering a market and don't have our own product to test yet.

Attention & findability

Can users see it and find it?

AI eye tracking

Predicts where users look on a screen, giving objective data on what captures attention first.

I reach for it when design elements don't appear to stand out.

First-click tests

Shows where users click first when given a task, a strong signal for findability.

I reach for it when a key entry point needs to stand out on a busy page.

Five-second tests

Reveals what users notice and remember in the first five seconds of seeing a design.

I reach for it when a page has to communicate its value at a glance.

Navigation tests

Finds where users get stuck looking for information or finishing a task because the navigation is unclear.

I reach for it when users land in the wrong place or drop off mid-flow.

Structure & preference

How do users think it should work?

Preference tests

Compares design options to confirm which one aligns with what users prefer, and why.

I reach for it when a team is split between two directions.

Design surveys

Qualitative questions about how users perceive specific design decisions.

I reach for it when we want reactions from more users than sessions allow.

Card sorts

Users group and label information in ways that make sense to them, revealing their mental models.

I reach for it when content is being reorganized or new sections are added.

Tree tests

Checks how well a site or app's structure matches how users expect to find things.

I reach for it when a new structure needs validating before any design is built.

Make insight travel

How do findings reach decisions?

Video storytelling

Short reels of real users that bring their voices straight into stakeholder discussions.

I reach for it when a finding needs to change a plan. Watching users struggle persuades more than my summary.

Research repository

Every session goes into Condens, tagged with a shared library of 250+ tags, so findings can be found and reused.

I reach for it when a stakeholder's question may already be answered by past research.

Journey mapping

Dynamic journey maps show where users struggle, and product teams can file Jira tickets straight from a pain point.

I reach for it when a problem spans several steps or teams, like registration abandonment.

Working with AI

How I move faster without losing rigor

Organizing evidence

First-pass highlight summaries, grouped observations, and questions across sessions in Condens.

I reach for it when I'm working through many sessions and want a starting point to check against the recordings.

AI-moderated research

Tools like Outset and Great Question run interviews and tests without a live moderator.

I reach for it when the questions are well defined and we need speed or breadth.

Repository search

AI-assisted search, including Condens in Slack, lets stakeholders find past evidence without needing a researcher to retrieve it. This roughly saved 20 minutes per request.

I reach for it when a question may already be answered by past research.

Structured review workflows

Workflow skills that my team developed review transcripts and recordings, propose potential findings, draft reports, assemble highlight reels, and check findings against source evidence, with researcher review built in.

I reach for it when a study needs to move faster and the evidence can be checked at each step.

Competitive and secondary research

AI helps synthesize reviews and compare existing experiences.

I reach for it when we need landscape context before or alongside primary research.

Where I draw the line

An AI theme or summary is never a final finding. I check it against the evidence, look for contradictions, and use the study context to decide what it means. AI-generated statements aren't participant evidence, and automated checks are never the only basis for approving a report. Participant information is handled before AI processing, and identifying details stay out of shared outputs.

Moderated usability studies moved from roughly 3–4 weeks to 1.5–2 weeks, and competitive and secondary research from 2–3 days to about one day.
